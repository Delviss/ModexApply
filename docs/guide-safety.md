# Student guides: onboarding and safety

What a guide is for, what they may not do, and what happens when they do it
anyway. This is the material a guide reads before their first conversation, and
the list Trust moderates against.

## What a guide is

A current student at a verified institution, who has proved they study there.
They are paid by Modex for delivering a session, and **never** by a student.

A guide answers the question a prospectus cannot: what the halls are actually
like, whether the library is open at 2am, how far the walk is in February, what
the course is like to sit through.

## What a guide is not

- Not an agent. They do not "handle" an application.
- Not staff. They do not speak for the university.
- Not a decision-maker. They do not influence admission.
- Not paid by outcome. The reward depends on the session being delivered and
  on nothing else — there is no column in the database joining a payout to
  whether a student was admitted, and that is deliberate.

## Out of scope: topics a guide must not advise on

This is the operative list. Anything here gets the same answer: *"I can't
advise on that — here's where to ask."*

| Topic | Where it goes instead |
|---|---|
| Visa eligibility, applications, refusals, appeals | The government's own service, and a regulated adviser |
| Immigration status, right to work, dependants | A regulated immigration adviser |
| Whether a specific applicant will be admitted | The university's admissions office |
| What to write in a personal statement, in their words | The student writes their own; a guide may say what theirs was like |
| Financial advice, loans, currency transfers | A regulated adviser; Modex's own price panel for fees |
| Medical, legal or mental-health advice | The university's services, and emergency services where relevant |
| Accommodation contracts and deposits | The university's accommodation office |
| Anything involving money changing hands | Nothing. Report it |

## The four rules that end an account

1. **Never ask a student for money.** Not a fee, not a deposit, not "to cover
   costs". Guides are paid by Modex.
2. **Never promise an outcome.** No guaranteed admission, no guaranteed visa,
   no "I can get you in".
3. **Never claim to be staff.** You are a student. Saying otherwise is
   impersonation.
4. **Keep the conversation on Modex.** Off-platform messages are not checked
   and cannot be reviewed if something goes wrong.

Messages are scanned as they are sent. A critical finding — a payment demand, an
impersonated admissions officer — suspends the account immediately, with no
human step, and opens a case with the message preserved as evidence. Everything
below that is flagged for a human.

## What a student sees

Every conversation carries a permanent safety banner that cannot be dismissed.
A flagged message is shown to the student **with the warning above it and the
message intact** — nothing is silently removed, because a platform that hides a
scam attempt teaches nobody what one looks like.

## Verification, and losing it

Guides prove they are current students through a university-domain email
challenge, a student ID document, or an institution roster. Evidence expires.
Thirty days out, a warning; at expiry, restriction; and the directory stops
showing them. Reverifying restores it.

Changing which university or programme you study repeatedly forces
reverification — three changes in six months. Scammers rotate institutions;
real students very rarely do.

## If something goes wrong

Report it, from any message or profile. Reports go to Modex Trust, not to the
university and not to the other person. Reporting somebody never reveals to
them that you did.

Guides can report students too. A guide being pressured for off-platform
contact, or for something on the out-of-scope list, uses the same button.
